Exercise 3: Labour Market Flows

The exercise presents three different methods for examining labour market flows - ie the transitions between one kind of involvement in paid work (for example, full-time employed) to another one (for example, economically inactive) using longitudinal data from the Labour Force Survey.

The estimates in this exercise relate to figures published by ONS on the ‘Labour market flows NSA’ sheet in Table X02. NSA stands for ‘non seasonally adjusted’, as we will be examining flows that took place between two quarters (as opposed to seasonally adjusted yearly averages).

Learning outcomes:

  • Becoming familiar with Two Quarter Longitudinal Labour Force Survey data
  • Being able to estimate labour market flows using different methods
  • Being able to use longitudinal weights

Data

This exercise relies on the Two-Quarter LFS Longitudinal Dataset for July - December 2024, which is available from the UK Data Service (Study number 9348).

  1. What is the FLOW variable?

  2. Why do some variables have a suffix of 1 or 2?

    1. FLOW measures labour market flows, that is the transitions between economic activity statuses Categories relating to labour force gross flows
  2. The suffix in most variables indicates the wave ie the quarter at which it was recorded.

Method 1: Using the FLOW variable

In order to match the definition used by the ONS for their published estimates, we will examine the FLOW variable but only consider respondents aged 16-64. We will then weight the variable with the longitudinal weight LGWT24. In addition to adjusting for non-response and sampling error, longitudinal weights also correct for respondents who drop out from the survey between waves.

As before, we first create a variable flagging the subpopulation of interest, here those aged 16-64.

dataex3<- dataex3 |> mutate(
                     wrkage=ifelse(AGE2>=16 & AGE2<=64,"16-64","Other"),
                     wrkage=as.factor(wrkage)
                      )

We then a copy of the working dataset that takes weights into account.

dataex3.s<- dataex3|> 
            as_survey_design(weights=LGWT24)

And finally, we compute the estimates.

t<-
dataex3.s |> 
            group_by(FLOW,wrkage) |>
            summarize(Counts = survey_total()
            )

Method 2: Employment situation at W1 and W2

Here, we create a variable to flag a group of respondents based on their situation at the first and second quarters.

dataex3 <-

Method 3: Cross-tabulation

This time we will be looking at transitions between employment broadly defined and self-employment from wave 1 to wave 2.
In order to do this, we will create a simplified economic activity variable recording employees (including those on training schemes as well as unpaid family workers) the unemployed, and the economically inactive at waves 1 and 2.

dataex3 <- dataex3 |> 
            mutate(
            eco1=case_when(
                     INCAC051=="[1] Employee" | INCAC051=="[3] Government emp & training programmes" | 
                     INCAC051=="[4] Unpaid family worker" ~ "Employee+",
                     INCAC051=="[2] Self-employed" ~ "Self-employed",
                     INCAC051=="[5] ILO unemployed" ~ "Unemployed",
                     .default="Inactive"
                     ),
              eco1=as.factor(eco1),
              eco2=case_when(
                     INCAC052=="[1] Employee" | INCAC052=="[3] Government emp & training programmes" | 
                     INCAC052=="[4] Unpaid family worker" ~ "Employee+",
                     INCAC052=="[2] Self-employed" ~ "Self-employed",
                     INCAC052=="[5] ILO unemployed" ~ "Unemployed",
                     .default="Inactive"),
               eco2=as.factor(eco2)
                     )

Let’s now get the results by using a cross-tabulation of economic activity at time 1 and time 2. We will rely again on wrkage to limit the analysis on the 16-64 as well as the longitudinal weights. The easiest way of doing this is to create a three-way table, which will in effect create a flow table for the 16-64 and another one for the other respondents.

We will first need to recreate the weights-informed dataset as we created new variables since the last time.

dataex3.s<- dataex3|> 
            as_survey_design(weights=LGWT24)

r<- svytable(~eco1+eco2+wrkage,
          design=dataex3.s
            )

What were the flows (ie number of people transitioning) between:

  1. Employment and self-employment
  2. Self-employment to employment?

  1. There were approximatively 194,372 people moving from employment to self-employment between July-August and September-December 2024.
