Exercise 3: Labour Market Flows

This exercise presents different methods for examining labour market flows - ie the transitions between one kind of involvement in paid work (for example, full-time employed) to another one (for example, economically inactive) using longitudinal data from the Labour Force Survey - in this case the 2 Quarter LFS.

Some of the estimates used in this exercise relate to figures published by ONS on the ‘Labour market flows NSA’ sheet in Table X02. NSA stands for ‘non seasonally adjusted’, as we will be examining flows that took place between two quarters (as opposed to seasonally adjusted yearly averages).

Learning outcomes:

  • Becoming familiar with Two Quarter Longitudinal Labour Force Survey data
  • Being able to use longitudinal weights
  • Being able to compute proportions with their confidence intervals
  • Being able to estimate labour market flows using different methods

Data

This exercise relies on the Two-Quarter LFS Longitudinal Dataset for July - December 2024, which is available from the UK Data Service (Study number 9348).

  1. What is the FLOW variable?

  1. FLOW measures labour market flows, that is the transitions between economic activity statuses

Method 1: Using the FLOW variable

In order to match the definition used by the ONS for their published estimates, we will use the exi]sting FLOW variable but only consider respondents aged 16-64. We will then weight the data with the longitudinal weight LGWT24. In addition to adjusting for non-response and sampling error, longitudinal weights also correct for respondents who drop out from the survey between waves:

  • We begin by creating a variable flagging the subpopulation of interest, here those aged 16-64.
dataex3<- dataex3 |> mutate(
                     wrkage=ifelse(AGE2>=16 & AGE2<=64,"16-64","Other"),
                     wrkage=as.factor(wrkage)
                      )

Method 2: Cross-tabulation

This time we will be looking at transitions between employment broadly defined and self-employment from wave 1 to wave 2.
In order to do this, we will create a simplified economic activity variable recording employees (including those on training schemes as well as unpaid family workers) the unemployed, and the economically inactive at waves 1 and 2.

dataex3 <- dataex3 |> 
            mutate(
            eco1=case_when(
                     INCAC051=="Employee" | 
                      INCAC051=="Government emp & training programmes" | 
                     INCAC051=="Unpaid family worker" ~ "Employee+",
                     INCAC051=="Self-employed" ~ "Self-employed",
                     INCAC051=="ILO unemployed" ~ "Unemployed",
                     .default="Inactive"
                     ),
              eco1=as.factor(eco1),
              eco2=case_when(
                     INCAC052=="Employee" | 
                     INCAC052=="Government emp & training programmes" | 
                     INCAC052=="Unpaid family worker" ~ "Employee+",
                     INCAC052=="Self-employed" ~ "Self-employed",
                     INCAC052=="ILO unemployed" ~ "Unemployed",
                     .default="Inactive"),
               eco2=as.factor(eco2)
                     )

Let’s now get the results by using a cross-tabulation of economic activity at time 1 and time 2. We will rely again on wrkage to limit the analysis to the 16-64.

What were the proportion of people transitioning between:

  1. Employment and self-employment
  2. Self-employment to employment?

  1. Between 0.5 and 0.9 percent of employees moved to self-employment between July-August and September-December 2024.

Method 3: Employment situation at W1 and W2

Here we examine the situation of respondents at the first and second quarters and create a variable identifying a transition of interest - whether a new job was started between Quarter 1 and Quarter 2.

dataex3 <-
dataex3 |> mutate(NewJob=
                  ifelse(ILODEFR2=="In employment" & 
                         EMPLEN2=="Less than 3 months" 
                         & AGE2>15 & AGE2<65,
                         "New job at Q2", 
                         "Anything else"),
                  NewJob=as.factor(NewJob)
                  )

dataex3.s<- dataex3 |> as_survey_design(weights=LGWT24)
